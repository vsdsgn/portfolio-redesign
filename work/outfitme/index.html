Case 04 / 04OutfitMeAI product

OutfitMe: Let the model help. Never lose the photo.

A solo, AI-native wardrobe and outfit builder. Photos become a structured wardrobe; the wardrobe becomes outfits. In active development and tested on device.

2 daysOne feature, plan to shipped

01Situation

Every AI step in a wardrobe app can fail. The user shouldn’t be the one who pays for it.

Turning photos into a usable wardrobe is a chain of fragile steps: capture, background removal, classification. If any of them blocks the save or throws the photo away, people stop adding clothes.

02Role / scope

Role
Solo — product, design and build
Product
AI-native wardrobe and outfit builder
Pipeline
Photo → wardrobe
Status
Active development, device-tested

03Decisions

  1. Save first, enhance later

    The item is stored the moment the photo is taken. AI enhancement runs afterwards and never blocks the save.

  2. Fallback keeps the original

    If background removal fails, the original photo stays in the wardrobe. A plainer image beats a lost item.

  3. Taxonomy constrains the AI

    The model classifies into a fixed wardrobe taxonomy — a closed set of answers instead of open-ended guesses.

  4. Short loops, real devices

    One feature went from plan to shipped in 2 days, and the app is tested on device, not only in a simulator.

06Throughline

Constraining a model with a taxonomy is the same move as constraining growth with a metric set: fix the boundaries, then move fast inside them. Which is where this started.
